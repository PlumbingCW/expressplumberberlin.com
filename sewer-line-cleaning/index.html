When the main sewer line is blocked

A blocked main shows up at the lowest fixtures first, usually a basement floor drain, a shower or a toilet. Water you add upstairs has nowhere to go. Stop using water, keep dishwashers and washers off, and call the 24/7 line. A licensed plumber will tell you what to avoid while a crew is on the way.

The fix runs through the main cleanout, a capped pipe with a square or round plug, often in the basement near the foundation or in the yard. Knowing where it is helps. If you cannot find it, we look for it when we arrive.

What to do while you wait

  • Stop all water use.
  • Do not run a washer or dishwasher.
  • Keep people away from wet floors.
  • Do not open the cleanout cap yourself, sewage can rush out.
  • Take photos if water has reached finished areas.

How we clean a main line

We open the cleanout and feed a heavy cable machine with a cutting head into the pipe. The cutter breaks up grease, paper and roots. When the line flows, we run water and check the drains again. If the cable meets a hard stop, the camera or a locator helps us decide what is happening.

For heavier buildup, a follow-up with hydro jetting scours the walls. If the line keeps clogging, a camera inspection shows whether the pipe is damaged.

Causes of main line clogs in older neighborhoods

Roots, grease, paper products and flushable wipes are the main causes. Wipes labeled flushable do not break down like toilet paper, and they bind with grease. Older cast iron collects scale, and clay joints let roots in.

In Berlin, a house in Kensington or East Berlin may be on a different line type than a newer home near the Turnpike. Pipe age matters more than the street name, so the camera is the only reliable guide.

Keeping the line clear

Avoid flushing wipes, paper towels, feminine products and grease. Do not pour cooking fat down the kitchen sink. Plant trees away from the line, and ask us for a camera look if you plan to build near it.

If your line clogs more than once a year, ask about scheduled cleaning or a repair. Repeat emergency cleaning costs more over time than fixing the cause.

Cleaning versus repairing: how to tell

A line that clears fully and stays open for a year or more has had a clog. A line that clogs again within months has a reason. Roots, a belly, an offset joint or scale are the usual reasons, and each needs more than cleaning. We say honestly when we think a camera look is the next step.

We also tell you when cleaning is all that is needed. Some lines only had a hair, paper or wipe blockage, and paying for repair would waste money.

Businesses and multi-family buildings

In a building with several units, a main line clog affects everyone, and the owner needs a plan. We coordinate with the manager, tell tenants what to avoid and clean the line from the building cleanout. We note the likely cause so the owner can address it, whether that is grease in a restaurant unit or wipes from the upper floors.
