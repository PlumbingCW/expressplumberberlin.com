Why plan ahead for a new water heater

Most water heaters are replaced under pressure, because they fail on a cold morning. A planned installation avoids that. You can compare fuel types, check electrical capacity and confirm that a power-vent or condensing unit will fit in the room before anyone starts cutting pipe.

Planning also gives time to fix problems that a rushed job skips, such as seized shutoff valves, corroded unions, a missing expansion tank or a vent that no longer meets what a new unit needs. These are small items when scheduled and expensive when found at midnight.

Choosing a unit type

Standard tank

The familiar choice. Lower equipment cost and simple service, but limited hot water per hour and standby heat loss.

Hybrid heat pump

Pulls heat from surrounding air. Needs clearance, a condensate drain and a space that stays above freezing.

Gas tank

Fast recovery. Needs a safe vent, combustion air and a tested gas connection.

Electric tank

Simple installation where gas is absent. Needs the right breaker and wiring for the element load.

Installation steps from start to finish

  1. Site check

    We confirm the room, the existing vent or chimney, the gas line size or the electrical panel.

  2. Isolate and drain

    Water and fuel are shut off and the old unit is drained.

  3. Set and connect

    The new unit is leveled, connected with new flexible connectors or piping and fitted with a relief valve and discharge line.

  4. Fuel and venting

    Gas joints are leak tested. Electric connections are checked against the unit rating.

  5. Test and explain

    We check hot water at a fixture, set the temperature and show you shutoffs and the drain valve.

Safety items that belong on every installation

What a safe installation includes

  • A temperature and pressure relief valve with a discharge pipe to a safe place.
  • A drain pan with a drain line where the heater sits above finished space.
  • A gas shutoff and a drip leg on gas lines.
  • Proper venting for gas and oil units, with a combustion air plan.
  • Carbon monoxide alarms near fuel-burning appliances.

These are not extras. They are the parts that protect your house and your family if something goes wrong. We install them, and we explain where each one is so you can find it later.

Setting the temperature

The temperature setting affects safety, comfort and energy use. A very hot setting raises the risk of scalds, especially for children and older adults. A very low setting can allow bacteria to grow in the tank. Many homes settle in a middle range, and a plumber can help you choose based on your household.

We set it before we leave, and we show you the control. If you have an unusual need, such as a dishwasher that needs hotter water, tell us.
