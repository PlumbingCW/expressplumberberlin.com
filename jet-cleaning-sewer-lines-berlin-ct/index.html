Jet Cleaning Sewer Lines in Berlin, CT

Jet cleaning uses high-pressure water to scour a sewer line. It is a strong tool for grease and scale, and a bad idea on a broken pipe. This guide explains the difference.

How jet cleaning works

A jetting machine pumps water through a hose with a nozzle that has several jets. Forward jets pull the nozzle along the pipe. Rear jets push debris back toward the access point. The water scours the full wall of the pipe, not just a channel through the middle like a cable does.

Pressure and flow are matched to the pipe. A small household line takes a lower setting than a large commercial line. The operator watches the hose and the response of the line and stops if something seems wrong.

Jetting compared with cabling

For a single clog, a cable is quick and enough. For grease, scale and repeat clogs, jetting leaves the pipe cleaner and the interval between problems longer. Jetting does not repair a broken pipe.

Why a camera comes first

A camera shows the pipe before pressure is applied. A crack, an offset joint or a section of collapsed clay could be damaged by high-pressure water or could let water escape into the soil. Seeing the pipe first avoids that risk.

The camera also decides whether jetting is the right tool. A pipe with a belly or a break will keep clogging until it is fixed. See sewer line repair for those cases.

What happens at the visit

  1. Locate the cleanout

    We use the main cleanout or another access point.

  2. Camera look

    We check pipe condition and note any damage.

  3. Jet in stages

    Pressure is raised in steps as the pipe allows.

  4. Verify

    We run water, review the camera again if needed and explain results.

Expect a mess only if the line vents into a floor drain. We protect the area and tell you what to expect. After the job, you should know what came out and whether to schedule a repeat.

When jetting is worth it

Jetting fits restaurants and kitchens with heavy grease, lines with scale in older cast iron, and lines that clog every few months with no sign of damage. Many homeowners use it once after a bad clog to reset the line, then change habits.

Jetting does not suit a pipe that has failed. For routine cleaning without the full jetting approach, see sewer line cleaning.

What comes out of a jetted line

After a first jetting, many homeowners are surprised by what the water brings back. Grease that has hardened into a waxy coat, grit and scale from old cast iron, and fine roots can all appear. Seeing it helps you decide what to change. If the debris is mostly grease, a change in kitchen habits matters. If it is scale, the pipe is aging and a camera look will tell you how far.

We describe what we saw, and we avoid scare tactics. A line that comes back clean and flows well is good news, and we say so.

Questions to ask before you book

Ask any company that offers jetting

  • Will you look with a camera before and after?
  • What pressure and nozzle will you use on my pipe size and material?
  • Do you have an access point, or will you need to pull a toilet?
  • What will you do if you find damage?
  • Will you tell me what came out and what to change?

A company that answers these clearly is more likely to protect your pipe. Be wary of anyone who promises that jetting will fix a line that has already broken.

Commercial and heavy-use lines

Restaurants, bakeries and laundries put more grease and lint through their drains than a home does. Many schedule jetting every few months instead of waiting for a backup. A schedule is cheaper than an emergency during a dinner rush, and it is easy to adjust once you see how fast the line fills.

Our commercial plumbing page explains how we plan service for kitchens, and the homeowner lines in this guide follow the same logic at a smaller scale.

How often is too often?

A line that needs jetting more than twice a year probably has a problem that jetting cannot cure. Roots at a joint, a sag that holds water or a section of damaged pipe will keep catching debris. At that point, the money is better spent on a camera look and a repair than on a third cleaning.

On the other hand, a restaurant line may need jetting several times a year as routine maintenance, and that is normal for grease-heavy use. Ask what is typical for your type of property.

Older pipe, public rules and permits in Berlin

Berlin dates back to 1785, and the age of a home usually tells you what kind of pipe to expect. Whether your home sits on the public sewer or a septic system is a question for the town offices, and cleaning methods differ for each. Jetting is for sewer laterals, not septic tanks.

Plumbing work in Connecticut needs a state license, and the Department of Consumer Protection has a lookup. We also work in Newington and Middletown, where older lines are common.
