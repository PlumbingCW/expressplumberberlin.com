When a hidden leak is an emergency

A leak is an emergency when water is actively spreading, when a floor feels warm or soft, or when the meter keeps moving with every fixture off. Shut off the main water valve if you can reach it, and call the 24/7 line. We will say what to check while you wait.

Even small leaks matter. A pinhole in a copper line can soak insulation and drywall for weeks before a stain shows. Mold, swollen trim and a musty smell are late signs. The earlier the source is found, the smaller the repair.

What to do first: quick, safe checks

  • Turn off every fixture and read the water meter, then read it again after twenty minutes.
  • Look for damp spots on ceilings, baseboards and basement walls.
  • Listen for running water when the house is quiet.
  • Check toilets with a few drops of food coloring in the tank.
  • Compare this month's water bill with the same month last year.

Signs of a hidden leak

Most homeowners find leaks through a high water bill, a damp patch, low pressure or the sound of running water. A hot spot in a slab floor can point to a leaking hot line. A bill that jumps with no change in habits is worth a meter check.

Some symptoms mimic leaks. A running toilet can add a surprising amount to a bill without any wall damage. A failed irrigation valve or an outdoor spigot can hide outside the house. We check the simple causes first, because finding a worn toilet flapper is a better result than opening a wall.

How we find the leak without opening everything

We start with a meter and pressure test. They show whether the leak sits on the supply side, in a branch or in the drain. Acoustic listening gear then picks up the sound of water escaping from a pressurized pipe. Moisture readings and thermal views help narrow the area, and we mark the likely spot before cutting any drywall.

Once the source is found, we explain the repair, whether it is a section of pipe, a fitting or a larger problem such as aging supply lines. If the pipe is failing in several places, repiping may be discussed. For a line between the street and the house, see water line repair and replacement.

Water meter and high-bill leaks in Berlin

Berlin is served by more than one public water system, so your meter reading and billing depend on which supplier serves your street. State records list the Berlin Water Control Commission, Kensington Fire District and Worthington Fire District among the community systems. Ask your own utility how to read your meter and what it considers a leak adjustment.

If your bill jumped, read the meter at night, wait for a quiet hour and read it again. Movement means water is going somewhere. We then separate the house from the service line and test each side.
