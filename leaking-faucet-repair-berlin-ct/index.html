Leaking Faucet Repair in Berlin, CT

A dripping faucet wastes water and annoys everyone. Most leaks come from a small worn part. This guide shows how to tell which, and what repair fits.

Find where it leaks

Dry the faucet and watch. A drip at the spout means the valve is not sealing. A leak at the base of the handle means worn O-rings or packing. Water under the sink may be a supply line or a drain connection that is not the faucet at all. Fixing the right part is most of the job.

Faucet types and their parts

Compression

Two handles with rubber washers. Washers wear and seats corrode.

Cartridge

One or two handles with a cartridge that controls flow. The cartridge wears and can be replaced.

Ball

A single lever with a ball and springs. Common in older kitchens.

Ceramic disc

A pair of ceramic discs. Durable, but mineral buildup can stop them sealing.

Knowing the type tells you which parts to buy. Parts differ by brand and model, and a wrong cartridge will not fit.

A basic repair, safely

  1. Close the supply

    Shut the valves under the sink. If they do not close, use the main.

  2. Open the faucet

    Let the line drain and plug the sink drain.

  3. Take it apart

    Remove the handle, the cap and the cartridge or stem, noting the order.

  4. Replace worn parts

    Match the part, clean the seat and lubricate O-rings with plumber grease.

  5. Reassemble and test

    Open the valves slowly and check for leaks.

If the faucet is old or corroded, parts may not be available or the body may be too damaged. That is when a new faucet is the better choice.

Repair or replace

Replace a faucet when the body is corroded, the finish is flaking, parts are no longer sold or you have repaired it more than once. A new faucet is also a chance to add shutoff valves and new supply lines. We install faucets you supply or help you choose.

If water is also under the sink, check the traps and supply lines. See drain cleaning if the sink is draining slowly.

Water and mineral buildup

Mineral scale clogs aerators and wears cartridges. White crust on the spout is a sign. Soaking the aerator in vinegar clears light buildup. If scale is heavy, test your tap or read your water supplier's annual report to see what is in the water.

A leak that you ignore wastes water, stains the sink and can damage the cabinet below. Fix it early.

Working out what to buy before you start

Most faucets have a model name or number on the body, the box or the underside of the sink. Write it down. If you cannot find it, remove the handle and take the cartridge or washer to a plumbing supplier. Matching a part by eye is how people end up with three wrong parts.

Take photos of the faucet from several angles before you disassemble it. They help you put it back in the right order, and they help a plumber if you give up and call. Keep small parts in a cup so they do not go down the drain.

Mistakes that make a leak worse

Common mistakes

  • Overtightening a handle or nut until the seat cracks.
  • Using the wrong cartridge because it looked similar.
  • Forgetting to close the shutoff valves first.
  • Skipping plumber grease on O-rings so they tear on assembly.
  • Ignoring the supply lines, which may be the real leak.

If you reach a point where parts do not fit or the faucet body is cracked, stop. A plumber can replace the faucet, and the cost of a new faucet is often less than the cost of another failed repair.

Saving water while you decide

A faucet that drips once a second can waste a surprising amount of water in a month, and the money shows on a water bill. While you wait for a repair, place a small container under the drip to catch it for plants, and close the valves under the sink if the leak is steady. A short term fix is better than nothing.

If you have a leak you cannot find, see leak detection. For a faucet that will not stop at all, call for emergency faucet repair.
