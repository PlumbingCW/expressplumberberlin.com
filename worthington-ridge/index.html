What Worthington Ridge is

The Worthington Ridge Historic District was listed on the National Register of Historic Places on July 13, 1989. It is a linear district of about 54 acres, running mostly along Worthington Ridge Road from Mill Street (Route 372) to Sunset Lane. A few properties on Farmington Avenue, Sunset Lane and Hudson Street are included.

The listing describes a mix of residential architecture and several of the town's civic buildings. Most residences are wood frame, and a significant number date to before 1820. The styles include Colonial, Late Victorian and Mid 19th Century Revival.

The district is a historic place and not a modern subdivision. Many people who live nearby use the name loosely, so call and give us your street if you are not sure whether you are on the ridge.

Plumbing in an older house

The age of a house tells you very little about its pipes. Many older homes have been repiped, added onto and updated more than once. We look at what is actually there before we recommend anything.

Drains

Older houses often have added bathrooms and kitchens tied into existing lines. A slow drain is a drain cleaning call, and a camera look through sewer inspection shows what the line really is.

Supply lines

If you have low pressure or repeated leaks, we look at the supply piping before suggesting repiping. Not every old house needs it.

Hot water

Tank age, venting and space all matter in a house that was not built for a modern heater. See water heater replacement.

Fixtures

Original sinks and tubs can be worth keeping. We can usually repair a leaking faucet or toilet without replacing the fixture.

Work in a historic district

National Register listing is an honor. By itself it does not stop a private owner from doing work with private funds, but the town may have its own review rules for exterior changes. Interior plumbing is rarely a historic review question, while a new vent, an exterior hose bib or a meter relocation can be.

Before any job that touches the outside of the house, ask the Town of Berlin whether any review applies. We confirm permit needs with the building office before work starts.

Questions to settle before plumbing work on an older home

  • Is any part of the work visible from outside, such as a vent or an exterior line?
  • Does the house have a known or suspected shared or old service line?
  • Are there fixtures or finishes you want to keep?
  • Will walls or plaster need to be opened to reach pipes?
  • Is a permit needed, and who pulls it?

Why the ridge is a good place to ask first

The 2013 town plan proposes treating the corridor from the New Britain line through Worthington Ridge as a commercial and cultural center. Whatever happens there, your plumbing questions stay practical. Where is the main shutoff, what is the pipe material, and who pays when a shared line fails.
