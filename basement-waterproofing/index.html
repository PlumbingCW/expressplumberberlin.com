Find the source before choosing a fix

Not every wet basement is a foundation problem. Water on the floor can come from a leaking supply line, a failed drain, a condensation problem or a gutter that dumps beside the wall. A true waterproofing job starts by ruling out plumbing causes, because a pipe leak is a simple fix and a drainage system for the wrong problem wastes money.

We check the plumbing, the sump if there is one, the gutters and downspouts and the grading near the foundation. If the water is entering through the wall or the floor joint, interior drainage can manage it. We explain what we see and what each fix does and does not solve.

How interior waterproofing works

An interior system catches water where it meets the footing and routes it to a sump pit. A channel is set along the base of the walls, below the floor surface, and the water drains into the pit. A pump moves it outside. The system does not stop water from reaching the foundation. It gives the water a controlled path so it does not spread across the floor.

  1. Open the floor edge

    A section of slab along the wall is cut and removed.

  2. Install channel

    A drain channel is set on the footing with gravel around it.

  3. Connect to the sump

    The channel leads to a sump pit with a pump and check valve.

  4. Restore the floor

    New concrete is placed over the channel and the floor is finished.

Interior systems are common because they do not require digging around the house. An exterior approach can address the wall directly but needs excavation and is a different trade in many cases. We tell you when a job is beyond what we do.

Sump pump and backup power

The pump is the heart of an interior system. A backup matters, because storms often bring outages. We discuss battery backups and alarms and what each costs and needs. For pump repair and replacement, see sump pump installation and repair.

Discharge should go away from the house and not toward a neighbor. We check the route and what freezes in winter. Berlin requirements for discharge and any permits are confirmed with the town before work starts.

Realistic expectations for older Berlin homes

Older foundations, such as stone or block walls in homes built long ago, can be damp in a wet spring even when nothing is failing. A dry basement may not be possible without major work, and a good plumber tells you that. We aim to control water, protect finished space and keep stored items dry.

If you plan to finish a basement, address water first. Carpet, drywall and wood over a damp slab invite mold. Humidity control and good drainage together give the best result.

What a walkthrough looks at

On the first visit we look at the wall, the floor and the joint where they meet, and we note stains, efflorescence, cracks and where water shows up after rain. We look at the sump pit if there is one, the downspouts outside and the grade of the soil. We ask when the water appears and how much, because the pattern tells us the cause.

We end with a plain description of what we found, what options exist and what each would do. If we think a landscaper or a mason should come first, we say that too.

Safety while the basement is wet

What to do when water is on the floor

  • Stay out of standing water if outlets, cords or the panel are near it.
  • Do not run a vacuum, space heater or power tool in a wet basement.
  • Move stored items and photograph damage for your insurer.
  • Turn off the supply valve if a pipe is leaking.
  • Call a plumber for pump or pipe problems and an electrician if the panel is wet.

Water and electricity are the real danger in a flooded basement. If you are unsure whether it is safe to go down, it is not. Call first and wait for guidance.
