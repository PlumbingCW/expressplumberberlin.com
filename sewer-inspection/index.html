What a sewer camera inspection shows

A camera head on a flexible rod travels down the line through the cleanout or a removed toilet. A screen shows pipe material, joints, roots, scale, offsets and low spots. A locator can mark the position of any problem above ground, so repairs begin at the right spot.

The inspection also confirms what is not wrong. A clean, round pipe with tight joints tells you a backup was a clog, not a failure. That finding can save a homeowner from an unneeded repair.

When to schedule an inspection

Good reasons to look inside the line

  • Repeat clogs in the main line.
  • Slow drains in several fixtures at once.
  • Sewage odors with no visible cause.
  • Buying an older home with an unknown sewer line.
  • Before planting trees or building near the line.
  • After a repair to confirm the work.

Older Berlin homes are a common reason for pre-purchase inspections. Many houses in Kensington and East Berlin have lines from earlier decades, and the camera gives a buyer real information before the closing date.

What you receive after the inspection

We review the footage with you, explain each finding and note the distance from the access point to each problem. You receive a plain description of the pipe condition and a recommendation. If the line is sound, we say so. If it needs work, we describe the options and the price before anything starts.

Footage can help when you talk with a seller, an insurer or the town. We describe what we saw. We do not offer legal or real estate advice.

Sewer connections in Berlin

Whether your home connects to a public sewer or a private septic system varies by street. Ask the Berlin town offices to confirm the connection type. The camera shows the pipe material and the condition of the lateral, but it does not show the town's records.

Our camera work covers the private side of the line. If the problem lies at or beyond the connection, we explain who is responsible and who to contact.

Reading the footage together

A sewer camera shows a pipe from the inside, and it can look alarming at first. A film of grease, a few water marks and a gentle curve are normal. A black gap at a joint, a visible root mass or a pipe that changes shape signals real trouble. We pause on each finding and explain what it is and how serious it is.

Ask questions as the camera moves. A plumber who watches the screen with you is more likely to give an honest read than one who looks at it alone and hands you a conclusion.

Using the report with sellers and insurers

If you are buying a home, the inspection can support a request to the seller to repair a defect or adjust the price. The finding should be described in plain terms, with distance and depth. We do not advise on contract language, but we give a factual description of the line.

For an insurance claim, a description of the damage and its likely cause helps the adjuster. Policies vary on sewer lines, so ask your insurer what they need before you schedule work.

Cleanouts: the small fixture that saves money

A properly placed cleanout gives access to the main line without pulling a toilet. If your house has none, we can install one, usually near the foundation wall or in the yard. It pays for itself the first time a line needs cleaning or a camera look, because access is faster and cleaner.

We install cleanouts with a threaded plug, set at a convenient height and clear of landscaping. You should know where yours is, and it should not be buried or painted shut.

Codes and licensing

Drain, waste, vent and fixture rules for work like this follow the plumbing provisions of that code.
