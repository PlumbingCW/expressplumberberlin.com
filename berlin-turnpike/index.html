A corridor, not a neighborhood

The Berlin Turnpike is a multi-lane divided road with mixed uses. It also runs through neighboring towns, and this page covers the Berlin stretch only. It is a commercial corridor and not a single residential subdivision, so the plumbing questions here are mostly about businesses and multi-unit buildings.

The 2013 town plan names retail plazas on the corridor and lists residential projects along it. The 2023 plan identifies the Turnpike and Farmington Avenue as corridors for denser housing where infrastructure supports it.

Plumbing for businesses and plazas

Commercial plumbing differs from a house in scale and in rules. Several tenants may share a building, a restroom may serve the public, and a restaurant adds grease and heavy drain use.

Drains and grease

Floor drains, restroom lines and kitchen drains are cleared through drain cleaning and hydro jetting when a line keeps coming back.

Hot water

A failed heater in a shop or office is covered under water heater repair and replacement.

Fixtures

Running toilets, leaking faucets and failing flush valves are repaired through toilet repair and faucet repair.

Commercial work

Larger jobs and tenant questions fall under commercial plumbing.

Many commercial buildings have backflow preventers that need periodic testing. Your water provider or the town tells you the schedule and who may perform the test. We can tell you whether a job you are planning touches one.

Condo communities on the corridor

If you live in a condominium community, your association decides who handles a shared line. A leak in a wall, a backup in a stack or a failed shared water heater is usually a question for the association first.

Unit owners can call us for problems inside their own unit once the association confirms the boundary. Keep the association rules and any shared-line diagram handy when you call.

Before you call about a multi-unit property

  • The property address and the unit or suite number.
  • Who owns the line, the owner, the tenant or the association.
  • Whether the property manager has approved the visit.
  • Whether water is shut off for one unit or the whole building.
  • Contact for building access.

Codes and licensing

Connecticut's State Building Code, effective October 1, 2022, adopts the 2021 International Plumbing Code (IPC). Drain, waste, vent and fixture rules for work like this follow the plumbing provisions of that code. Permits and inspections in Berlin Turnpike are handled by the local building office, and we confirm what a job needs before it starts.
