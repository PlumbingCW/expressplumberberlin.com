Who we are

Berlin Express Plumbing handles repairs, installations and emergency calls for homes and businesses in Berlin and nearby Central Connecticut towns. Our address is 1195 Farmington Ave, Berlin, CT 06037, in Kensington, and calls are answered by licensed plumbers.

We keep the process simple. You call, we explain what to do, we look at the problem and we give you a clear price before work starts. You decide whether to go ahead.

How we work

Plain explanations

We describe the cause in everyday words and show you what failed.

Clear price first

No work starts until you agree to the price.

Honest options

We tell you when a repair is not worth it, and when replacement is.

Local habits

We know Berlin's older homes, Turnpike businesses and Route 9 commuters.

Connecticut requires a state license for plumbing work. The Department of Consumer Protection lets you verify any plumber's license online, and we encourage you to check anyone you hire.

What we do not do

We do not take on jobs outside our trade. Boiler burner work, electrical service and structural carpentry belong to other licensed trades, and we say so. We do not post reviews or ratings here, because we would rather you read the work described on each service page than a list of numbers.

We do not promise arrival times. We promise a clear conversation and a plan.

How a call becomes a repair

  1. You call the 24/7 line

    A licensed plumber answers, asks what is happening and tells you what to shut off.

  2. We plan the visit

    We explain what we think we will find and when a crew can arrive. We do not promise arrival times.

  3. We look at the problem

    On site, we find the cause and show you what failed.

  4. You approve a clear price

    We explain the options in plain words and give you a price before work starts.

  5. We repair, test and explain

    We test the work and tell you what to watch for afterward.

That sequence is the whole business. It sounds simple because it is, and the hard part is doing it the same way on every call, including the late ones.

Where we work and why that matters

Our shop address is on Farmington Avenue in Kensington, which is also Route 372, and it meets Route 71 at the center of Kensington. Route 9 bypasses the village to the north and east, and US Route 5 runs through town as the Berlin Turnpike. Those roads are how crews reach most of our calls, and they are why Newington, Cromwell and New Britain are close neighbors.

Berlin is a Hartford County town that was incorporated in 1785. It has historic landmarks such as the Worthington Meetinghouse and the Kensington Congregational Church, and modern places such as Berlin High School and the Berlin station. Those are the landmarks our crews drive past, and they remind us that a lot of the housing here is older and has its own plumbing habits.

Water comes from more than one public system. Connecticut Department of Public Health records list the Berlin Water Control Commission, Kensington Fire District and Worthington Fire District for the town. We do not tell you what your water is like, because it depends on your street. We tell you to ask your utility or test your tap.

Licensing and how to check it

Connecticut licenses plumbing work through the Department of Consumer Protection, with separate license types for journeyperson plumbers and plumbing contractors. The department has a public lookup where you can verify any license by name or number. We encourage you to look up whoever you hire, including us.

We do not list license numbers on this page, because they belong in your quote and your invoice where you can compare them with the state record. If a plumber will not give you a license number, that is your answer.

How we price

We give you a clear price before work starts. The price depends on the problem, the access and the parts, so we do not post numbers online that would not match your house. Each service page lists the factors that move the price, so you know what to expect.

We do not charge for a conversation on the phone, and we will tell you if a repair is not worth doing. If we recommend replacement, we show you why.

What you can expect from us on site

Crews introduce themselves, wear covers over their boots in finished spaces and put down protection where they work. They explain what they find before they touch anything, and they ask before shutting water off to a household that may be in the middle of something.

When the job is done, they clean up, show you the repair and tell you what to watch for. If something looks wrong after they leave, you can call the same number and a licensed plumber will answer.

Codes and licensing

Connecticut's State Building Code, effective October 1, 2022, adopts the 2021 International Plumbing Code (IPC). Drain, waste, vent and fixture rules for work like this follow the plumbing provisions of that code. Permits and inspections in Berlin are handled by the local building office, and we confirm what a job needs before it starts.
