Tub Faucet Leak Repair in Berlin, CT

A tub faucet that drips, or a shower that will not stop trickling, usually has a worn valve inside the wall. This guide explains the causes and the repair.

Where a tub faucet leaks

A tub faucet has a valve inside the wall, a handle on the face and a spout that sends water to the tub. A drip from the spout when the valve is closed means the cartridge or washer is worn. A leak around the handle means worn seals. A leak in the wall, noticed as a stain on the ceiling below or damp drywall, means a failed connection or a pipe.

Because the valve sits in the wall, even a small leak can be hidden. Pay attention to a damp smell, a soft spot in the wall or a stain on the floor below.

The diverter problem

The diverter is the part that sends water to the showerhead instead of the spout. On a tub with a pull-up knob, the diverter sits in the spout. When it wears, water leaks from the tub spout even when the shower runs. A new spout often fixes it.

On a tub with a three-handle valve, the diverter is a separate valve in the wall. Replacing it needs access, and a plumber can do it behind an access panel if one exists.

Safe checks for homeowners

What to do first

  • Look for the drip with the valve fully closed.
  • Check whether the shower or tub spout is the source.
  • Look under the tub from the room below for a stain.
  • Check the caulk at the spout and the tub wall.
  • Avoid forcing a stuck handle.

Do not pry the spout off the wall without knowing what holds it. Some spouts slip on, others screw on, and a wrong approach can damage the pipe inside.

What the repair looks like

  1. Shut off the water

    Use the home shutoff, or the valve supplied for the bath.

  2. Open access

    A panel behind the wall in an adjacent room, or a small opening at the valve.

  3. Replace the cartridge or valve

    The part is matched to the brand and model.

  4. Reseal and test

    The spout is reset, caulked and tested with the tub and shower.

If the valve is old and parts are no longer made, a replacement of the whole valve is the repair. That involves the wall and usually tile work, and we tell you before starting.

When the leak is behind the wall

A stain on a ceiling below the bathroom, damp drywall or a musty smell calls for testing. A pressure test and moisture check can show whether a supply connection, the tub drain or the valve is leaking. See leak detection. For a burst or fast leak, see burst pipe repair.

The earlier the leak is found, the smaller the repair. Mold behind a wall can spread quickly.

When tile and the wall are involved

Some tub valves can be reached through the spout side or through a panel on the other side of the wall. Others, especially in tiled walls with no access, require cutting a hole. A neat opening can be patched with a cover plate or tile, and a plumber should tell you where the cut will be and who will finish it.

If the walls are being replaced anyway, the best time to renew a valve is before the new tile goes up. A new valve with a new cartridge costs little compared with what you would pay to open a finished wall later.

Mold, moisture and when to act fast

A tub faucet that has leaked into a wall for a long time can leave wood damp and drywall soft. Signs include a musty smell, paint that bubbles, grout that stays wet and a ceiling stain below. Fix the leak first, then dry the area, and replace materials that stayed wet.

If the damage looks large, a restoration company can dry the structure. We handle the plumbing side so the repair is ready before the drying starts.

Safety in a wet bathroom

Water near electrical fixtures is the main hazard. If a ceiling light below the bathroom is wet or sagging, do not turn it on. Switch off the circuit if you can reach the breaker safely and call.

Do not stand under a ceiling that is bulging with water. Poke a small drain hole only if a plumber advises it, and place a bucket under it.

Code and licensing for bathroom work in Berlin

Opening a tub wall and replacing a valve is plumbing work. Connecticut requires a state license for it, and the Department of Consumer Protection has a lookup. Connecticut's State Building Code, effective October 1, 2022, adopts the 2021 International Plumbing Code.

Permit rules for valve replacement are set by the town. Berlin's building department can tell you what applies. We also serve Newington and Middletown for bathroom plumbing.
