Plumbing help in Kensington

Kensington is a village on the west side of the Town of Berlin. Berlin Station sits in the Kensington neighborhood, and the Kensington Congregational Church still stands as one of the village landmarks. Town sources do not draw a firm boundary around the village, so this page uses anchor streets instead.

Farmington Avenue, Kensington Road and Percival Avenue all appear in the village history. If you are not sure your street counts as Kensington, call and give us the address. We will tell you whether we can help.

Drains and sewer lines

Slow sinks, backed-up tubs and floor drains are covered under drain cleaning, with a camera look through sewer inspection when the cause is unclear.

Water heaters

No hot water, leaks at the tank and noises are covered under water heater repair and replacement.

Leaks and shutoffs

Hidden leaks and a high water bill start with leak detection. A burst pipe starts with the main shutoff.

Basement water

A sump pump that fails in a storm is a sump pump job, and a wet basement wall is a waterproofing question.

What is changing in the village center

Berlin has zoned a Kensington Overlay Zone to promote a transit-oriented, pedestrian-friendly, village-type environment. According to the 2023 regional study, the highest-density village zone allows mixed-use residential at up to 20 dwelling units per acre.

The same study describes Steele Center on Farmington Avenue, planned with 76 market-rate apartments and about 19,000 square feet of retail, restaurant and medical office space. The town plan also notes that housing in Berlin clusters near village centers such as Kensington Center.

That mix matters for plumbing. A single house has its own lines. An apartment building or condo shares stacks, drains and sometimes a water heater. A leak or backup in one unit can start in a shared line, so the owner or the management company usually decides who gets called.

Water near the Mattabesset River

The Mattabesset River limits the buildable area near Berlin Station. The 2023 regional study recommends a 20-foot buffer from the river to reduce flood risk. The 2013 town plan lists flooding along Farmington Avenue as a priority.

That does not mean any particular basement floods. It does mean a working sump pump is worth checking before a storm. Pour a bucket of water into the pit and watch the pump start and empty it. If you live near the river, ask the town for the flood zone that applies to your address.
