How to Repair a Boiler: What Berlin, CT Homes Need to Know

A boiler that stops in winter is urgent, but most repairs are not DIY. This guide shows which checks are safe, which faults are common and when to call a licensed plumber.

Safety first

If you smell gas, leave the building and call the gas utility or 911 before you do anything else. If you smell oil fumes or see smoke or soot around the boiler, shut it off and call. A boiler holds hot water under pressure, and opening the wrong part can scald you. The rule is simple: look, note and check, but leave cutting, welding and gas work to a licensed professional.

Checks any homeowner can make

  • Is the thermostat set above the room temperature and powered?
  • Is the boiler switch on and the breaker up?
  • Does the pressure gauge read in the normal range given in your manual?
  • Is there fuel in the tank or gas at the appliance?
  • Is there any water on the floor around the boiler?

Bleeding air and topping up

Air trapped in a radiator or a baseboard loop can leave a room cold. Many systems have bleed valves on the radiators or at a high point. Open a bleed valve slightly with the boiler off and the system cool, and close it when water flows. Then check the pressure gauge and add water through the fill valve if the manual allows.

If the pressure drops again quickly, there is a leak. Do not keep adding water, because fresh water brings oxygen that rusts the system. A plumber can find the leak and test the expansion tank.

Zone valves and circulators

In a system with zones, each zone has a valve or a circulator that sends hot water to its loop. A failed zone valve leaves one area cold while the rest heats. A failed circulator can leave the whole house cold while the boiler runs. These parts are standard replacements, and a plumber can swap them with the system drained or isolated.

Tell the plumber which zones work and which do not. It narrows the cause quickly. If a pipe has frozen or burst, see burst pipe repair.

Repair or replace

A part is a repair. A boiler that leaks from its body or has a cracked section is at the end of its life. We help compare repair with replacement, with a price for each.

A pre-season service in the fall catches many problems before they turn into a no-heat call. Fuel supply, venting and controls should be part of the check.

Preparing for the cold season

A boiler that works in March can fail in November after months of rest. Before the first cold snap, check the pressure, look for drips at valves and fittings, and listen for unusual noise on the first run. Bleed any radiators that have been cold or noisy.

Have the fuel supply confirmed. An oil tank should be filled and the filter changed on the maker's schedule. A gas boiler needs a clear vent and combustion air. Keep the area around the boiler free of storage, because a boiler needs air and space to work safely.

Protecting the pipes if the heat goes out

If the boiler stops during a freeze, the pipes in outside walls are at risk. Open cabinet doors under sinks, let a faucet drip slightly, and close off unused rooms to keep the warmest parts of the house livable. Use space heaters only as the maker directs, and never use a gas oven for heat.

If you will be away, ask someone to check the house daily in cold weather. A frozen pipe that bursts can do more damage than the boiler failure that started it. If a pipe does burst, see burst pipe repair.

Licensing and permits for boiler work in Connecticut

Connecticut licenses plumbers and other trades through the Department of Consumer Protection, which has a lookup for license verification. Boiler and fuel work can involve a different license category than plumbing, so verify the license that matches the job.

Permit rules are set by the town. Berlin's building department can tell you what a boiler replacement needs. We also serve homeowners in New Britain and Newington, where many older houses heat with boilers.
