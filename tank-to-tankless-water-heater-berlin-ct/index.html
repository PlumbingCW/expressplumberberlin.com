Replacing a Tank Water Heater with a Tankless Unit in Berlin, CT

Going from a tank to a tankless heater changes the gas, the vent, the plumbing and the way you use hot water. This guide walks through the swap so you know what to expect.

Decide with real numbers

A tankless heater costs more to buy and to install, and it can use less energy because it does not keep a tank hot. The saving depends on how much hot water you use, the price of fuel and the cost of the upgrades the house needs. A household that uses little hot water may never earn back the extra cost.

We prefer to compare both options on your house. We ask about the tank age, fuel, venting and the number of people. Then we describe each path and its price, and you choose. If your tank is leaking now, replacement on the same fuel may be the faster path, with tankless planned later.

What changes in the house

Gas supply

A larger line may be needed to feed the burner.

Venting

Tankless units often use plastic vent pipe or a sealed side-wall vent.

Condensate

High-efficiency units need a drain for acidic condensate.

Electrical

A nearby outlet powers the controls and fan.

Water piping

Isolation valves and a pressure relief setup change the layout.

The old flue and the old tank location may no longer be useful. Some homeowners move the heater to a wall closer to the bathrooms, which shortens the wait for hot water.

The swap, step by step

  1. Walkthrough and sizing

    We check fuel, gas line, venting route and flow needs.

  2. Remove the tank

    Water and gas are shut off, the tank is drained and removed.

  3. Install the unit

    The tankless heater is mounted, vented and connected to gas and water with service valves.

  4. Condensate and controls

    The drain, power and controls are connected.

  5. Test and explain

    We test flow, set temperature and show you how to flush it.

A straightforward swap takes a day. Gas line or vent changes can add time. We tell you the plan before starting.

Living with tankless

You will not run out of hot water in a long shower, but you may notice a short delay at first and a cold pocket if you open and close the tap quickly. Very low flow can keep the burner from firing, so a faucet barely open may stay cool.

Plan to flush the unit on schedule. Replace the inlet filter screen. A service plan is wise where scale is a concern. Keep the vent and the condensate drain clear.

When to stay with a tank

A tank is simpler and cheaper to install, and it works well when hot water use is modest and the house has no room for a vent. Hybrid heat pump tanks are a third option and fit homes with space and a warm room. Our water heater installation page covers tank choices.

If your tank has failed and you need hot water now, see emergency replacement.

Questions to settle before the swap

Settle these first

  • Does the gas meter and line have the capacity for a tankless burner?
  • Where will the vent exit the house, and does it meet clearance rules?
  • Is there a drain nearby for condensate?
  • How many fixtures run at the same time in your busiest hour?
  • Who will flush the unit, and how often?

If any answer is unclear, a walkthrough clears it up. We would rather postpone a swap than install a unit that cannot perform.

Planning the timing

The best time for a swap is before the tank fails. A planned day lets you choose the unit, schedule the gas work and avoid cold showers. If the tank is already leaking, a quick like-for-like replacement may be the right first step, with tankless planned for later when the weather and your budget allow.

Summer is often easier for gas and vent work, because you are not without heat if a utility visit takes longer than planned. Winter swaps are possible, but they leave less margin if something unexpected turns up.

What the first few weeks feel like

Expect small adjustments. Some fixtures may need a minute to deliver hot water, especially the ones farthest away. Shower valves with very low flow may need a slight change of setting. Write down anything odd and tell the installer, because most quirks are settings, not defects.

Run a hot tap before a shower to flush cooled water from the line. If you notice a smell or hear a rumble, call for service. A new unit should be quiet and steady, and an installer who stands behind the work will come back to adjust it.

Primary-source facts for a Berlin swap

Connecticut's State Building Code, with its 2022 edition effective October 1, 2022, adopts the 2021 International Plumbing Code, and gas and vent work follows additional rules. A swap involving a new gas line or vent may need a permit, and the Berlin building department can tell you what applies.
