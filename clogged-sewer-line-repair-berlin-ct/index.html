Clogged Sewer Line Repair in Berlin, CT

A clogged main sewer line stops every drain in the house at once. This guide covers the signs, the first steps and how to tell a clog from a failing pipe.

Signs the main line is the problem

One slow sink is a local clog. A main line problem shows up in several fixtures at once. A toilet that gurgles when the washer drains, a basement floor drain that bubbles when the upstairs shower runs, and several slow drains at the same time all point to the main. Sewage odors near a floor drain are another sign.

The lowest fixture in the house usually shows trouble first, because that is where backed-up water emerges. In many Berlin homes that is a basement floor drain or a first-floor shower. If you see water coming up, stop using water everywhere in the house.

First steps

  • Stop all water use, including washers and dishwashers.
  • Keep people and pets away from wet floors.
  • Do not pour chemical drain cleaner into a backed-up line.
  • Find the main cleanout, but do not open it yourself if water is high.
  • Call a plumber and describe which fixtures are affected.

Clog or broken pipe?

A true clog is made of grease, paper, wipes or roots. A broken pipe has a physical defect, such as a crack, an offset joint or a bellied section, that catches material again and again. Cleaning a damaged pipe gives temporary relief. Fixing the pipe gives lasting relief.

How the line is cleared

We open the main cleanout and feed a cable machine with a cutting head into the pipe. When the line flows, we run water and check each fixture. If the cable cannot pass, or if the clog returns, a camera shows what is blocking the pipe. For heavy buildup, hydro jetting scours the walls.

You should receive a clear explanation of what came out and what the camera shows. If the plumber cannot tell you what caused the clog, ask for a camera look before paying for repeat cleaning.

When repair makes sense

Repair is worth discussing when a line clogs more than once a year, when the camera shows damage or when roots keep returning. Options include a spot repair at a single defect, lining of a long damaged run and replacement where the pipe has failed. We explain which fits your line and what each involves.

A repair involves digging or lining and is a bigger decision than cleaning. We give a clear price before work starts, and we do not push repair when cleaning will do. For the full picture, see sewer camera inspection.

Keeping a clean line clean

Do not flush wipes, even those sold as flushable. Keep cooking grease out of the sink, and use drain strainers in showers and tubs. Plant trees away from the sewer line and ask for a camera look before building near it.

A line that has been cleaned and found sound can often go years without trouble. A line with damage will keep clogging until it is repaired.

What a repair estimate should include

After a camera look, a plumber should be able to tell you where the problem sits, measured from the cleanout, and how deep. That distance and depth drive the cost of any repair. Ask for the footage and a description of what the camera saw, in words you can follow.

A fair estimate also names the method and why it fits. Lining is right for a long run with small cracks and a round pipe. A spot repair fits one short defect. Replacement fits a collapsed or badly offset line. If the plumber cannot explain why a method suits your pipe, ask again or get a second opinion.

Restoration is easy to forget. Digging up a lawn, a walk or a driveway leaves a surface to repair, and who does it should be stated up front.

Protecting your home while you decide

If a repair is not scheduled right away, protect the house in the meantime. Limit water use, spread laundry over several days and avoid running the dishwasher and washer at the same time. Place a plug or a cap on the basement floor drain if a plumber advises it, and keep an eye on it.

Know the warning signs. A gurgle, a slow drain and a smell near a floor drain mean the line is filling. If sewage appears, stop using water and call the 24/7 line, then see our page on sewer line cleaning for what happens next.

Sewer connections and responsibility in Berlin

Whether a Berlin home connects to a public sewer or a private septic system depends on the street, and the town is the place to confirm it. The Berlin town offices can tell you where your connection sits and who is responsible for each part of the line.

If you are not sure, a camera inspection shows the private side. Our crews also serve homeowners in Newington and Middletown, where the same questions come up.
