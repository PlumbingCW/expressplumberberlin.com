Cleaning Underground Gutter Drains in Berlin, CT

Many homes send downspout water through buried pipes. When those lines clog, water spills at the gutter or pools at the foundation. Here is how to find and clear the blockage.

What an underground gutter drain is

An underground gutter drain, also called a buried downspout line, carries roof water away from the house through a pipe below the lawn. It usually runs from each downspout to a pop-up emitter, a daylight outlet at a slope, a dry well or a storm drain. The pipe is often corrugated plastic or solid PVC.

These lines clog with leaves, shingle grit, pine needles and soil. Roots can enter at joints. In winter, standing water in a low spot can freeze and block the line. The first sign is overflow at the gutter, a downspout that backs up or soggy ground near the foundation after rain.

Find where the line goes

Before you flush anything, trace the line. Walk the yard and look for a pop-up emitter, a grated catch basin, a rock-filled dry well or a pipe that ends at a slope. Look for the cleanout caps, short vertical pipes with a plug, where the downspout enters the ground.

Flushing the line yourself

  1. Disconnect the downspout at the elbow

    Make sure the downspout itself is clear first.

  2. Flush with a garden hose

    Run the hose into the buried pipe and watch for the outlet to flow.

  3. Use a drain auger for stubborn clogs

    A light cable can break up debris and mud.

  4. Check the outlet

    Confirm water flows freely at the exit, then reconnect the downspout.

Do not use chemical cleaners. They will not dissolve leaves or soil, and they can damage plastic pipe. If the hose water backs up or the cable stops hard, the pipe may be crushed or full of soil.

When to call a plumber

Call when the line is crushed, when roots have grown in or when the line has no cleanout and you cannot reach the blockage. Hydro jetting can clear packed debris from a buried pipe. A camera shows whether the pipe is intact. See hydro jetting and camera inspection.

We keep gutter drain work separate from sewer work. Roof water should not go into the sanitary sewer, and we do not connect it there. If you suspect a mix-up, a camera and a dye test can show where each line goes.

Reducing future clogs

Install gutter guards or clean the gutters twice a year, especially after leaf fall. Add a strainer at the top of each buried line. Keep the emitter clear of mulch and leaves. Regrade soil that slopes toward the house.

A buried line that clogs often may have a belly. A short regrade or a replaced section solves it better than repeated flushing.

What to do after a heavy storm

A storm shows quickly whether a buried line works. Watch the downspouts during a heavy rain. If water spills over the gutter, a downspout fills and runs back up or the ground near an outlet turns to mud, the line is not keeping up. Take photos and note where water pools, because that tells a plumber where to look.

After the storm, check basement walls near the downspouts for dampness. A line that is blocked underground can push water against the foundation, and that is a more serious problem than a full gutter.

Keeping roof water and sewer water apart

Gutter lines and sewer lines are separate systems and should stay that way. Roof water is clean enough to go to a lawn, a dry well or a storm drain where allowed. Sewage must go to a sewer or septic system. Connecting roof water to the sanitary sewer can overload it in a storm and may break local rules.

If you inherited a house and are not sure where each line goes, a camera and a tracing dye can show it. Knowing which pipe is which keeps you from flushing the wrong line or paying for work that does not help.

Calling before you dig in Berlin

Any excavation near a buried downspout line should start with a call to 811, the national Call Before You Dig number, which has underground utilities marked before work begins. Gutter lines are private, but gas, water and power lines run nearby.

Rules on where roof water may be discharged can be set by the town, so ask the Berlin town offices before changing an outlet. We help homeowners in Cromwell and Southington with the same questions.
