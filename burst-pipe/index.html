When a burst pipe is an emergency: what to do in the first five minutes

A burst pipe is always an emergency. Close the main water shutoff valve, which is usually where the line enters the house near the meter. Open a faucet at the lowest level to drain the system. Call the 24/7 line, and a licensed plumber will guide you while a crew is on the way.

If water reaches outlets, appliances or the electrical panel, do not touch them. Stay out of standing water. If it is safe, switch off power to the wet area at the breaker. If you cannot reach the breaker safely, leave the area and call.

  1. Close the main valve

    Turn it clockwise. If it does not move, call and we will help.

  2. Drain the lines

    Open the lowest faucet and flush the toilets to relieve pressure.

  3. Cut power to the wet area

    Only if you can reach the breaker without standing in water.

  4. Move belongings and photograph

    Lift items off the floor and take pictures for your insurer.

  5. Call the 24/7 line

Why pipes burst in Connecticut

Water expands as it freezes, and the ice plug builds pressure between itself and a closed faucet. The pipe often splits where ice formed, but the break shows only after thawing. Pipes on exterior walls, in unheated crawlspaces and in garages are most at risk.

Not every burst is frost. Corrosion, high pressure, a failed fitting or a nail through a wall can cause a break. We repair the break and check the system pressure and the surrounding pipe so the problem does not return.

Frozen pipes: thaw before they burst

If a faucet gives only a trickle in freezing weather, a pipe may be frozen but not yet burst. Open the faucet, apply gentle heat from a hair dryer or warm towels along the pipe, and never use an open flame. If you cannot find the frozen section, call so we can locate it safely.

Thawing can reveal splits. Be ready to shut the main valve as the ice melts. If a pipe is already bulging or leaking, close the main first and call.

What the repair looks like

We cut out the damaged section, replace it with new pipe and fittings and test the line under pressure. If the break is inside a wall or ceiling, we open a small access, repair the pipe and tell you what drywall and painting remain. We look for other weak points, especially where the same run froze.

If the water damage is large, a restoration company dries the structure. We coordinate the plumbing side so the repair is ready before drying begins. For a longer-term answer to repeated failures, see repiping.
