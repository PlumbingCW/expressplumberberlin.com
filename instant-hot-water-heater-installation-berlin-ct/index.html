Instant Hot Water Heater Installation in Berlin, CT

Instant hot water heaters, also called tankless or on-demand heaters, heat water as it flows. Installing one is more than a swap. This guide covers what has to be in place.

What instant hot water means

An instant hot water heater has no storage tank. When a hot tap opens, water flows through a heat exchanger that a gas burner or electric elements warm on the way. Hot water arrives for as long as the flow lasts, and it stops when the tap closes. The phrase can be misleading, because the water still takes time to travel from the heater to the fixture.

Some people also call small point-of-use units instant heaters. Those sit under a sink and serve one fixture. This article covers whole-house units, the type that replaces a tank heater.

Sizing the unit

Tankless units are sized by flow and temperature rise. A household with two showers, a dishwasher and a washer running at once needs more flow than a couple. Groundwater temperature in winter is colder, so the heater must raise it more, and a unit that works in July may struggle in January.

We ask how many bathrooms, which fixtures run together and how much flow you expect. We then match the unit to the peak, not the average. A unit that is too small gives lukewarm water when demand is high.

Pros and trade-offs

Where tankless helps

  • No standby heat loss from a stored tank.
  • Smaller footprint on the wall.
  • Hot water as long as flow lasts.
  • Long service life when maintained.

Where to be careful

  • Higher equipment and installation cost.
  • May need gas or vent upgrades.
  • Needs flushing for scale.
  • Sensitive to low flow and poor water quality.

Neither type is right for every house. Our tank installation page explains when a tank still fits.

Maintenance after installation

Flush the heat exchanger on the schedule the maker and your water suggest. Clean the inlet screen. Check the vent and the condensate drain. Isolation valves make flushing quick, so we fit them on every new installation.

If the unit throws an error code, note it and call. Many codes point to flow, venting or scale problems that are repairable. For urgent loss of hot water, see water heater repair.

Point-of-use versus whole-house units

A small point-of-use heater sits under a sink and warms water for one fixture. It is cheap to install and ends the wait at a distant tap. It does not replace a main heater. A whole-house unit replaces the tank and serves every fixture, so it needs larger gas, venting and plumbing changes.

Many homes benefit from one or the other, not both. A bathroom at the far end of a long run is a good candidate for a point-of-use unit. A family that runs several showers at once is the case for a whole-house unit. We help you decide based on how the house is laid out.

The cost picture without guessing

Equipment, venting, gas line, electrical work, condensate drain, permit and removal of the old heater are the parts of the price. Each one changes with the house. We do not publish a single number because the range is wide, and a figure from another town would mislead you.

What we can do is give you a clear price after a walkthrough, in writing, with each part named. If it does not pencil out against a tank, we say so and help you plan the tank swap instead.

Safety points for any new heater

What to do before and after installation

  • Make sure a carbon monoxide alarm is working near fuel-burning appliances.
  • Keep the area around the heater and its vent clear of storage.
  • Learn where the gas shutoff, water shutoff and breaker are.
  • Do not use a heater with a gas smell or scorch marks, leave and call the gas utility.
  • Check the temperature setting so hot water is safe for children and older adults.

A new heater should come with a short walkthrough of these items. Ask for one if it does not.

Berlin water, permits and gas

Scale is the main enemy of a tankless heat exchanger, and it depends on your water. Berlin is served by several public water systems, according to Connecticut Department of Public Health records, so check your own supplier's annual water quality report or test your tap. We do not quote a hardness figure for Berlin.

Whether a water heater installation needs a permit and an inspection is a town decision, so ask Berlin's building department. Neighbors in Newington and Cromwell often compare the same options.
