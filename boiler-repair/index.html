What to check when the boiler stops

Start with the simple checks. Is the thermostat calling for heat? Is the boiler switch on, and is there power? Does the pressure gauge show a normal reading, and is there fuel? If you smell gas, leave the building and call the gas utility or 911 before calling us. If you smell oil fumes, or the boiler smokes, shut it off and call.

Many boiler calls trace to low pressure, a failed circulator, a stuck zone valve or air in the system. A leak from the boiler body or a relief valve that drips is a more serious sign. We explain what is safe to do and what should wait for a technician.

Safe checks before calling

  • Check the thermostat settings and batteries.
  • Confirm the boiler switch and the breaker are on.
  • Read the pressure gauge and note it.
  • Look for water on the floor around the boiler.
  • If you smell gas, leave and call the gas utility or 911 first.

Repair or replace the boiler

A circulator, a valve or a gauge is a part, and part repairs are normal. A boiler with a cracked heat exchanger or a body that leaks has reached the end. We help you compare repair against replacement and describe the cost of each before you decide.

If you need domestic hot water from the boiler, a failing indirect tank is another candidate. For a standalone unit, see water heater repair. For a replacement boiler, we discuss sizing, venting and the heat distribution system.

Heating season in Berlin

Winter in Connecticut puts every heating system under load. A boiler that has been quiet all summer can fail at the first cold snap. A pre-season check of pressure, valves and the expansion tank can find problems early. Make sure the fuel supply and venting are clear.

Frozen pipes are a risk when a boiler fails. If the heat is out, keep the house above freezing if possible, open cabinets under sinks on exterior walls and let a faucet drip slightly. If a pipe has already burst, see burst pipe repair.

Maintenance that prevents no-heat calls

A yearly service before the heating season finds weak parts early. We check pressure, test valves, look at the expansion tank and listen to the circulators. A technician for the burner side should check fuel and combustion. Between services, glance at the gauge and look for water on the floor.

Keep a record of the date, the pressure and any parts replaced. If something changes, such as a new noise or a different pressure range, that record helps diagnose it quickly.

Codes and licensing

Connecticut's State Building Code, effective October 1, 2022, adopts the 2021 International Plumbing Code (IPC). Relief valves, discharge lines and connections on water heating equipment follow that code, and fuel, venting and burner work can fall under other licensed trades. Permits and inspections in Berlin are handled by the local building office, and we confirm what a job needs before it starts.
