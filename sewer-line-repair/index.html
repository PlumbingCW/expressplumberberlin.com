Sewer backup: what to do first

A sewage backup is an emergency for your health and your property. Stop using every fixture and keep people away from the affected floor. Call the 24/7 line, and a licensed plumber will tell you what to avoid while a crew is arranged.

We handle the cleaning and the cause. A cable or jet may restore flow, but if the camera shows a crack, a bellied section or roots at a joint, the real answer is repair. We show you the footage and explain what it means.

Common causes of sewer line damage

Roots

Tree roots seek water and enter at joints. They return unless the entry point is fixed.

Offset joints

Soil movement can shift pipe sections, leaving a lip that catches debris.

Belly or sag

A low spot holds water and solids and clogs repeatedly.

Collapse or crack

Old clay and cast iron can crack or crumble, and the line may need replacement.

Frost and thaw cycles in Connecticut move soil, and older pipe on shifting ground is more likely to separate. Heavy vehicles over shallow pipe can add stress. A camera inspection is the only way to know which problem you have.

Trenchless methods such as pipe lining reduce digging but are not right for every line. A collapsed section or a severe belly cannot be lined. We explain why one option fits and another does not, and you decide with a clear price in front of you.

Permits and utilities in Berlin

Replacing a sewer line may involve the town as well as the plumber. Berlin has a Building Department with online permit services, and we confirm what your job needs before starting. Work in a public street needs separate approval. Dig-safe notification for underground utilities is part of any excavation, and we handle that call.

If you are unsure whether your property sits on public sewer or a private septic system, ask the town. We can tell you what the camera shows, but the municipal records settle the connection question.

What happens on a repair day

  1. Call before digging

    Underground utilities are marked before any excavation begins.

  2. Protect the property

    We set up access, protect the yard and keep the work area clear.

  3. Expose or line the pipe

    Digging or lining begins at the marked defect.

  4. Replace or reline

    The damaged section is replaced or relined and connected to sound pipe.

  5. Camera check and backfill

    A camera confirms the repair before the trench is closed.

A camera look after the repair is the proof that the pipe is sound. We show you the footage and explain the finish, and we tell you what restoration is left, such as lawn repair or paving.

Living with the repair afterward

A repaired line should flow cleanly, but it still depends on what goes into it. Keep grease and wipes out of the drain, and keep roots in check by planting trees away from the line. If you plan any landscaping near the line, we can mark where it runs.

Keep the footage and any paperwork. They help if you sell the house or if an insurer asks what was done.
