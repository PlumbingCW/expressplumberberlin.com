Before We Arrive: If You Have A Burst Pipe Or Active Leak

The first minutes decide the damage. Do these four things in order.

Shut off the main valve

Turn the main valve clockwise where the line enters the house. For a failed heater alone, close the cold valve above the tank.

Kill power to wet areas

Stay away from standing water near electrical equipment. Use the breaker only if the panel is dry.

Clear the area

Move stored boxes and furniture off the basement floor, and photograph the damage before cleanup.

Basement water and sump pumps in Southington

Heavy rain and spring thaw are the stress tests for Southington basements. A sump pump that fails or a pit that overflows can flood finished space in hours. We repair and replace pumps and check valves, and we explain backup options. See emergency sump pump repair and basement waterproofing.

If the pump runs constantly in dry weather, a leaking pipe or high groundwater may be the cause. We check the plumbing first, because a pipe leak is a smaller fix.

Southington ZIP codes and the wider area

We cover Southington's main ZIP, 06489, and Plantsville, 06479, plus the roads that reach toward Berlin and Meriden. For a wider radius, our crews also reach Plainville and Kensington, and the most requested work there is water heater service and drain cleaning.

Planned jobs are welcome too. Ask about water heater installation or repiping for older homes.

Large lots, long runs

Southington has many larger lots than neighboring towns, and that changes the plumbing. Water lines from the street can be long, sewer laterals run farther and outdoor spigots and irrigation lines are more common. A long service line has more places to fail, and a long sewer line has more places for roots.

For these properties we start with the question of where the pipe runs and how old it is. Knowing that tells us whether cleaning, a camera look or a repair is the sensible next step. See water line repair and sewer camera inspection.

Codes and licensing

Connecticut's State Building Code, effective October 1, 2022, adopts the 2021 International Plumbing Code (IPC). Drain, waste, vent and fixture rules for work like this follow the plumbing provisions of that code.
