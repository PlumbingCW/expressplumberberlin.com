When a faucet problem is an emergency

A faucet that will not close, a spray from a cracked supply line or a leak under the sink cabinet can flood a kitchen quickly. Turn the small valves under the sink clockwise. If they do not hold or do not exist, shut off the main water valve and call the 24/7 line. A licensed plumber will tell you what to do while a crew is arranged.

Old shutoff valves are a frequent hidden problem. Valves that have not moved in years can seize or leak when turned. We carry replacements, and we fit new ones during the repair so the next failure is easy to stop.

First steps

  • Turn the sink valves clockwise to close them.
  • If they fail, shut off the main water valve.
  • Place a bucket and towels under leaks.
  • Clear the cabinet so we can reach the valves.
  • Keep electrical items away from water.

Why faucets drip, leak and fail

A drip from the spout is usually a worn washer, cartridge or seat. A leak at the handle often means worn O-rings. Water under the sink may be a failed supply line, a loose connection or a drain leak that looks like a faucet problem. We check each connection before replacing parts.

Repair or replace

A single worn cartridge is a small repair on a faucet that is otherwise sound. A faucet with a corroded body, a finish that is peeling or parts that are no longer sold is better replaced. We tell you what we see and what each option costs.

When we install a new faucet, we check the holes in the sink or counter, the supply lines and the drain connection, and we test for leaks at full flow. Whole-home work such as new valves and supply lines can be added to the same visit. For bigger changes, see plumbing installation.

Kitchen and bath faucet types

Single-handle

One lever for flow and temperature. Common in kitchens.

Two-handle

Separate hot and cold valves. Common in bathrooms and older homes.

Pull-down kitchen

Spray head on a hose. Needs a counterweight and a clear cabinet space.

Touchless or smart

Adds a power source and sensors. More parts that can fail.

Spray hoses, aerators and the small things

Not every faucet leak is the main valve. A pull-down spray hose can crack at the fitting, a diverter can stick, and an aerator can clog and spray sideways. These are quick fixes when you know where to look. We check them before replacing a faucet, because the repair is usually small.

A faucet that sprays from the side or sputters often has scale in the aerator. Soaking it in vinegar clears light buildup. If the faucet still sputters, the cartridge or the supply line may be the cause.
