When a failed tank is an emergency

A tank that is leaking from its shell cannot be patched, and the water keeps coming until you stop it. Close the cold water valve on top of the tank, switch off the gas or the breaker, and call the 24/7 line. A licensed plumber will tell you what to do while a crew is arranged, and the same-day schedule starts with that call.

A water heater tells you before it fails, if you know where to look. Rust-colored water from the hot side only, a tank that rumbles loudly, moisture on the floor around the base and a supply valve that weeps all point toward replacement. When a tank leaks through its shell, nothing can be patched.

The data plate shows the manufacture date in the serial number. A tank in its second decade is a candidate for planned replacement before it chooses its own time. Planned replacement costs less in stress because you choose the day, and it avoids a flooded basement and wet drywall.

  1. Shut off the supply

    Close the cold water valve on top of the tank. Turn gas to pilot or the electric breaker off, depending on the unit.

  2. Call the 24/7 line

    Tell us the fuel type, the tank size on the label and where the water is coming from.

  3. We confirm the cause

    A loose fitting or relief valve is a repair. Water from the shell means replacement.

  4. You approve a clear price

    Fuel type, size and venting changes are named before any work starts.

Sizing, fuel type and venting

Replacing like for like is the simplest path. The old and new unit share a footprint, fuel, vent and connections. Changing fuel or type, for example from a tank to a heat pump unit, changes venting, electrical, condensate and sometimes floor space, so it belongs in a planned job rather than a midnight emergency.

Tank size follows household hot water use. A larger household with simultaneous showers and a dishwasher needs more first-hour capacity than a couple. We ask about people, bathrooms and appliances rather than assuming the old size was right.

What a clean replacement visit includes

We drain and disconnect the failed unit. We set the new one level on a pan or drain-friendly base where the space allows, make new water and fuel connections, and test for leaks. Gas work includes a leak check at every joint. Electric work includes a check of the breaker and wiring against the unit rating.

The old tank leaves with us. We test hot water at a fixture, check the temperature setting and the relief valve discharge line, and show you the shutoff and drain valve locations. Berlin permit rules for water heater work are set by the town, so we ask the Berlin Building Department before work and follow the answer rather than assuming.

Replace now or keep repairing?

A leaking shell is not a close call. The harder choice is an older unit with a small failure. If the unit has needed a part this year and sits near the end of its range, putting that money toward a new unit often makes more sense, because the next failure will arrive soon.

If the existing unit is still sound, our water heater repair page explains what we fix. If you are curious about on-demand heat, the tankless water heater page explains the trade-offs, including flushing needs.

Codes and licensing

Connecticut's State Building Code, effective October 1, 2022, adopts the 2021 International Plumbing Code (IPC). Relief valves, discharge lines and connections on water heating equipment follow that code, and fuel, venting and burner work can fall under other licensed trades. Permits and inspections in Berlin are handled by the local building office, and we confirm what a job needs before it starts.
