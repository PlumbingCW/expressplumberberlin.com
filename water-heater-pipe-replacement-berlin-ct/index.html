Water Heater Pipe Replacement in Berlin, CT

The pipes around a water heater take heat, pressure and corrosion. This guide explains which parts wear out and what replacing them involves.

What is around the heater

Cold supply and shutoff

The line that feeds the tank, with a valve to isolate it.

Hot outlet

The line that carries hot water to the house.

Relief valve and discharge

A safety valve with a pipe that carries any release away safely.

Gas or electric connection

A gas line and flexible connector, or electric wiring.

Expansion tank

A small tank that absorbs pressure from heated water on closed systems.

Each part ages. The fittings near the tank see the most heat and moisture, so they corrode first. A green crust on copper, a white crust on a union or a drip at a valve are signs.

Dielectric unions and corrosion

When two different metals meet in water, one can corrode the other. Dielectric unions or nipples isolate the metals, for example a steel tank fitting from a copper pipe. When the isolating part wears out, corrosion eats the fitting and a leak starts.

If you see crust, rust or a green stain at the top of the tank, the connection needs attention. A drip may be small, but it can get worse without warning, and it can mean the fitting is nearly through.

When to replace the pipes

Replace connectors and valves when you replace the heater. It is a small cost, and it avoids a leak in the first years of the new unit. Replace them sooner if you see crust, drips or a seized valve. A shutoff valve that does not turn is a risk, because you cannot isolate the tank in an emergency.

If the pipes in the wall also show corrosion, ask about a wider repair. See repiping for when supply piping needs replacing as a system.

The relief valve line

The relief valve protects the tank from too much pressure or temperature. Its discharge pipe should run to a safe place, usually near the floor or to a drain, without a cap or a valve. A capped or blocked relief line defeats the safety.

If the relief valve drips, do not plug it. Call a plumber to find out why. High house pressure and a missing expansion tank are common causes. For failed or leaking tanks, see water heater replacement.

What a replacement visit includes

We shut off water and power or gas, drain the tank as needed, cut out the corroded pipes, install new pipe, new valves and dielectric connections, and test under pressure. A gas connector is replaced and leak tested. We check the relief line and the expansion tank.

The visit is usually quick on an accessible heater. Pipes that run into a wall or ceiling take longer. We explain what we find and the price before work starts.

Flexible connectors and why they age out

Flexible water connectors are braided hoses that join the tank to the house piping. They make installation easy, but the rubber inside and the braid outside age. Over time the braid can rust or fray, and the hose can split. A connector that looks fine from the outside can leak at the crimp.

Replace connectors whenever the heater is replaced and look at them each year. A connector with rust stains, a bulge or any weeping at the ends should be changed right away. Gas connectors follow the same rule and should be replaced only by someone who will leak test the joint.

When pipe replacement is part of a bigger picture

If the pipes near the heater are corroded, ask whether the rest of the system is in the same condition. Corrosion rarely stops at one fitting. A plumber can inspect exposed runs and tell you whether a local repair is enough or whether a larger project is worth planning.

For a planned project that replaces a long run of supply pipe, see repiping. For a leaking heater tank itself, see water heater replacement.

Safe habits around a leaking heater

What to do if you see crust or a drip

  • Close the cold water valve above the tank.
  • Switch the heater off at the breaker or set the gas valve to pilot.
  • Do not touch wet wiring or controls.
  • Place a pan under the drip and photograph the connection.
  • Call a plumber before turning it back on.

If you smell gas, leave the building and call the gas utility or 911 first.

Berlin water and code notes

Corrosion depends partly on the water. Berlin is served by several public water systems, according to Connecticut Department of Public Health records, so ask your supplier for its annual report, and test your tap if you notice crusting. We do not quote a hardness number for the town.

Connecticut's State Building Code, with its 2022 edition effective October 1, 2022, adopts the 2021 International Plumbing Code. Ask Berlin's building department whether pipe replacement at a heater needs a permit. We serve Cromwell and Southington as well.
